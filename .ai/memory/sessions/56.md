# Findings

1. High: a retransmitted old offer can remain queued after its answer makes the
   old PC stable. `canReplacePeerConnection` does not include `pendingOffer`,
   and replacement merely clears the field (`negotiate.ts` 173-199), while the
   ordered output retains and retries the actual event (`peersessions.tsx`
   165-185). Go can answer that duplicate old offer before receiving the fresh
   offer; the browser applies every answer to its current PC without transport
   or offer correlation (`negotiate.ts` 416-424). The old answer can therefore
   complete the fresh PC's pending offer with old Go ICE/DTLS credentials, and
   the later fresh answer fails in stable. The same-track/same-channel rebuild
   makes the old answer structurally compatible with the fresh offer.
2. High: changed-fingerprint replacement always wins a Go-side collision and
   discards Go's old pending offer (`negotiate.go` 457-482, 655-688), but a
   polite browser still yields to that delayed old offer as ordinary glare
   (`negotiate.ts` 385-438). With Go impolite and its old offer delayed until
   the browser fresh PC has its own local offer, the browser rolls back the
   fresh offer and answers the abandoned old one; Go's fresh answer then meets
   stable, and the answer to the old offer is invalid on Go's fresh PC. The Go
   collision test constructs the old offer but never delivers it to the remote
   fresh PC (`negotiate_test.go` 613-619).
3. Medium: browser rebuild changes the active sender but intentionally leaves
   callers with only their original handle (`peersessions.tsx` 385-391,
   746-749). `useCallMedia` continues to use that old sender for DTMF
   capability and insertion (`useCallMedia.ts` 364-380) after the old PC is
   closed (`peersessions.tsx` 751-753), with no sender-update notification.
   DTMF therefore remains tied to the replaced transport. Remove-by-old-handle
   works, but the other existing sender use does not.
4. Medium: an impolite browser that receives a channel before the matching
   local subscription never records its label: `onDataChannel` only calls
   `attach`, while `channelLabels.add` exists only in `createChannel`
   (`peersessions.tsx` 628-634). A later subscription sees the existing channel
   and skips `createChannel` (`peersessions.tsx` 1281-1291). Rebuild iterates
   only `channelLabels` (717-720), so the label is omitted. If another known
   channel gives the fresh offer an application m-line, Go correctly creates
   none itself and the omitted channel/waiter is not restored. The fresh-PC
   test subscribes before `ondatachannel`, so it cannot expose this ordering.

# Test/mock assessment

- The browser fresh-PC test (`peersessions.test.tsx` 673-755) uses only a local
  mock and an outgoing offer; it never performs a real or scripted fresh
  offer/answer exchange with Go. The two SDP-generation findings are therefore
  outside its execution path.
- The mock's `setRemoteDescription` accepts answers in every signaling state
  and performs no SDP matching (`peersessions.test.tsx` 370-374), unlike the
  real stable-state failure involved in both protocol findings.
- Pion source confirms that returning to stable re-evaluates negotiation-needed
  (`third_party/webrtc/peerconnection.go` 1124-1130), so no additional defect
  was confirmed for the polite-Go/no-application follow-up offer path.
- No other directly confirmed defect was found in fingerprint extraction,
  application m-line duplication avoidance, Go track/codec/context rebinding,
  stale local callback filtering, or restart/glare timer cleanup. Speculative
  old-track event ordering and style-only observations were excluded.
